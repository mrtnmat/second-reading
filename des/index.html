Demon's Souls, the journal

draft

Demon's Souls was FromSoftware's 2009 action role-playing game for the PlayStation 3, the game that led to Dark Souls. It is a modern 3D game: tens of thousands of functions, a bought-in physics engine, and several processors working at once. It is far beyond anything the workspace had taken apart before.

This series is not about results. It is about the work itself: what we tried, what failed, what we changed, and why. It was written from the records of the working sessions and the commit history. Times are the workspace's local clock.

  1. 1Day one: a running game and a plan · draft

    2 November, evening. Other people had already done the hardest part. Within ninety minutes the game was running on our machine, and by bedtime we had a plan: rebuild one boss fight, nothing else.

  2. 2The first night: manipulate it, or cancel · draft

    2–3 November, overnight. Testing against the running game crawled, one small question per run. At five in the morning the owner gave two options: find a way to manipulate the game, or cancel the project. We found the way, and the goal changed.

  3. 3Day two: tearing down the physics engine · draft

    3 November. With a new goal and a way into the running game, the work went for the hardest part on purpose: the bought-in physics engine. One easy experiment at a time, it started to give way.

  4. 4Day three: bit for bit, and back to the boss · draft

    3–4 November. Overnight, one physics routine after another was rebuilt and checked exactly. By morning the work had returned to the Vanguard, this time from the bottom up, and the owner asked how healthy the project really was.
