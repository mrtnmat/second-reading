Plain-language meanings of the words the articles use. Game-specific terms say which game they belong to.

Breakpoint

An instruction to an emulator to pause the game the moment it reaches a chosen spot in its program, so a script can look at what the game is about to compute.

Decompile

To turn a program's machine code back into readable source code, close to what the original programmers wrote. The result works the same way but has lost all its names; giving them back is most of the work.

Emulator

A program that imitates a games console on a modern computer, so the original game runs unchanged. Research emulators can also be paused, inspected and controlled by scripts.

EXP

King's Field. Experience points, earned by defeating monsters. Enough of them raise your level.

Function

A named piece of a program that does one job, such as "work out the damage of a hit". A game is made of thousands of them.

Ghidra

A free tool for reading machine code, made by the US National Security Agency and widely used for this kind of research. It includes a decompiler.

Integer division

Division that drops everything after the decimal point: 7 ÷ 2 is 3, not 3.5. Old consoles calculate this way because it is fast, and it explains many small oddities in game numbers.

Kanji

The Chinese-derived characters of written Japanese, one per word or idea. Ordinary text uses a few thousand of them, alongside the two syllable alphabets, hiragana and katakana (together called kana).

MAG

King's Field. The player's magic power. It decides how strong spells are and how fast the magic gauge refills, the way STR does for weapons.

Mednafen

An accurate open-source emulator of several consoles, including the PlayStation. We use a modified copy that our scripts can control.

Memory address

A number naming one spot in the console's working memory (RAM). The PlayStation has 2 MB of it, and a running game keeps everything there, such as the player's health at one address and their strength at another.

STR

King's Field. The player's strength. It raises the damage of weapon hits, how fast the swing gauge refills, and your defence against every kind of attack.

Strafe

To step sideways while still facing forward. In King's Field it is on the shoulder buttons.

Swing gauge

King's Field. The bar that refills after every weapon swing. How full it is when you attack decides how hard the attack hits.

Tile

One square of a map grid. In King's Field each floor is 100 × 100 tiles, each 2,000 of the game's distance units across.

Update

One step of a game's internal clock, in which everything moves, gets checked and is redrawn once. King's Field runs 20 updates per second.

Video memory

A console's separate memory for pictures: what is on screen, plus every texture and font waiting to be drawn. The PlayStation has 1 MB of it.
