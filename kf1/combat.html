How combat really works

King's Field, read from the code, part 4 · revised 2026-10-23 · draft

In short

Every hit in King's Field goes through the same small formula, whether you are hitting a monster or a monster is hitting you. A few things about it were impossible to know for sure from playing alone:

  • Strength works both ways. It makes your swings hurt more, and it also makes monsters' blows hurt you less.
  • Armour counts its slash protection twice. What looks like a slip in the original program makes every piece of armour twice as good against slashing as its own numbers say. The game's status screen has always shown the doubled number, so that is the number players have been writing down for three decades.
  • No armour is a wall. More protection always helps, but each extra point helps less than the one before.
  • Poison resistance is a plain percentage. A poison resistance of 30 means poison sticks 69 times out of 100. At 99 you are immune.

We know these things because we read the game's program and then checked the predictions in the running game. Every prediction we tested matched exactly.

How it works

Five kinds of damage

Every weapon has up to five attack values, one per kind of damage: slash, strike, thrust, holy and fire. A short sword is mostly a slashing weapon; an axe is mostly a striking one. Every monster has a defence value for each kind. When you hit something, the game works out the damage for each kind separately and adds the five results together.

The curve

For each kind, the game compares your attack with the monster's defence:

  • If your attack is higher, you get the difference, plus a bonus.
  • The bonus is big when your attack is high and the defence is low, and small the other way round. It shrinks as defence grows, but never quite to nothing (only rounding down can make a hit worthless).

So against a well-armoured monster you still chip away a little with every hit, and against a soft one damage climbs fast: roughly with the square of your attack once you are stronger than its defence in the code. Doubling a strong weapon's attack against a weak monster can more than double the damage.

The same curve runs the other way when a monster hits you, with your armour as the defence measured.

Strength, on both sides

Your strength (STR) is folded into the curve on both sides:

  • When you attack, every 5 points of STR adds one point of attack to each kind of damage your weapon already has. STR never adds fire damage to a sword that has none in the code.
  • When a monster attacks you, every 5 points of STR adds one point to every defence you have measured.

This second half surprised us. In our test, the same floor-1 monster (a Man Trap) that did 10 damage per hit to a new character did only 2 per hit when we raised the character's STR from 20 to 100, with no armour at all measured.

The swing gauge

The bar that fills up after you swing is the swing gauge. Its level at the moment you press attack becomes the swing's power: swing at half a gauge and you do half the damage measured. Magic always hits at full power measured.

How fast the gauge fills depends on your STR and on the weapon's weight. With 20 STR, a Short Sword refills in about 1.1 seconds; the heavy Dragon Sword takes about 7.8 seconds, and still about 2 seconds at 80 STR in the code. After each swing the gauge rests for half a second before it starts filling again measured.

When the blow lands, and how far it reaches

Pressing attack doesn't hit straight away. The game checks for a hit exactly once, about 0.55 seconds into the swing measured; one weapon, the Corissumaldo, checks after only 0.2 seconds in the code. At that moment it looks at a spot in front of you and asks whether a monster is close to it. How far ahead that spot sits is the weapon's reach: about a third of a floor tile for the Short Sword, and about two thirds for the longest weapons, the Crescent Axe and the Flame Sword in the code.

If the final damage rounds down to zero, the hit does nothing at all: no damage and no flinch in the code.

The armour quirk

When the game adds up your armour, it counts each piece's slash defence twice. Strike, thrust and the rest are counted once, and your weapon is counted normally measured. It looks like a copy-and-paste slip in the original source code our reading, but it is how the game has always played: equipping a Breast Plate whose own numbers are 1 slash and 2 strike gives you 2 and 2 measured.

Players had no way to see this. The item screens show no numbers, and the status screen shows the already-doubled total. Armour tables made by players match that screen, so they quietly list the doubled values our reading.

Status effects from hits

Some monsters can poison, curse, darken or paralyse you when they hit. First the monster rolls its own chance, then your protection gets a say measured:

  • Poison: your poison resistance is a straight percentage. At resistance 0 poison sticks almost every time; at 50 about half the time; at 99 or more never.
  • Dark: wearing the Moon Charm blocks it completely.
  • Curse lowers your STR by 20, which, as above, also lowers your defence against everything.

That last point can be dramatic. Strength counts toward defence, so a curse that takes a character's STR from 20 down to 0 leaves them with almost no defence against fire. In our test, a wizard's Fire Ball did 4,080 damage to such a character; the formula gives 152 without the curse measured.

Small things settled along the way

  • The Blood Mask costs 8 STR, not 8 HP as some guides say. Its HP drain is a separate effect measured.
  • The Light Ring adds 5 holy attack, the Moon Charm 7 dark-magic defence, the Wind Blade Bracelet 7 fire defence, and the Twin-Headed Dragon Ring 8 MAG measured.
  • Spells use your magic power (MAG) the way swings use STR measured.

From here on it gets technical: formulas, memory addresses and how each claim was checked.

The details

All values below are in the game's own units. Addresses are where things sit in the PlayStation's memory while the 1994 Japanese release runs. Labels: measured means we predicted the number from the code and then saw exactly that number in the running game, in the Mednafen emulator unless noted; in the code means it follows from the decompiled program but hasn't been measured; our reading is our interpretation.

Player hits monster

kf_DamageEnemy (8002d120) calls the shared curve FUN_8002d078 once per damage type. Everything is scaled by 10 first, and all divisions round down (integer division):

A = 10*attack + 2*STR        (0 if attack == 0)
D = 10*defence               (1 if defence == 0)
r = max(0, A - D) + A*A / (2*D)
damage = power * ((r_slash + r_strike + r_thrust + r_holy + r_fire + 5) / 10) / 5000

power is the swing gauge at the press (0–5,000; stored at 800a079a); spells pass 5,000. STR is the current, effective STR (800a07a6). The A*A / (2*D) term is the "bonus" above: it never reaches zero while A is non-zero, and it grows with the square of attack.

Worked example, measured: Short Sword (slash 2, strike 1, thrust 1), STR 20, full gauge, against the floor-1 Man Trap (defences slash 2, strike 10, thrust 3, holy 6, fire 2):

TypeADr
slash602040 + 90 = 130
strike501000 + 12 = 12
thrust503020 + 41 = 61
holy, fire00

(130 + 12 + 61 + 5) / 10 = 20, times 5000/5000: 20 damage, which is what the game does measured.

Monster hits player

FUN_80016324 uses the same curve with STR moved to the defence side:

A = 10*monster_attack        (0 -> nothing)
D = 10*player_defence + 2*STR   (1 if 0)
r = max(0, A - D) + A*A / (2*D)
damage = (((sum of the five r + 5) / 10) * k >> 12) * m / 10

The five pairs are slash, strike and thrust against the player's defences at 800a07bc/be/c0, and two magic types against dark-magic (800a07c4) and fire-magic (800a07c6) defence. A melee hit passes k = 0x1000, m = 10, so it reduces to (sum + 5) / 10. Spells pass the caster's damage multiplier m (the monster type record's +0x82).

The cursed Fire Ball: fire attack 10 (A = 100), m = 80. Uncursed, STR 20 and no fire defence give D = 40, r = 60 + 125 = 185, damage (190 / 10) · 80 / 10 = 152. Cursed, STR 0 gives D = 0, which the game treats as 1: r = 99 + 5,000, damage 510 · 80 / 10 = 4,080, as observed measured.

Recalculating your stats

kf_RecalcStats_guess (80015714) rebuilds attack and defence whenever equipment or status changes:

  • Attack (800a07b0..b8) is the weapon's five values only.
  • Defence sums head, chest, shield, arms and feet. In each armour slot's block (the chest's at 800158b0–800158dc), the slash value is loaded once and added to 800a07bc twice; the other types are added once. The weapon block has no doubling measured.
  • Modifiers: Blood Mask STR −8; curse (status bit 0x01) STR −20; the Resist Fire buff (bit 0x10) fire defence +10; accessories as listed above measured. STR and MAG cap at 999 in the code.

Checked by equipping twelve items through the real menu one after another: RAM defence always equalled twice the slash sum plus the rest measured.

Swing timing and reach

FUN_80016bc0 and kf_Attack:

  • While not swinging, the gauge (800a0798) rises by 2 * ((64*STR) / (weight + 1) + 1) per update, up to 5,000. weight is weapon byte 1 (Short Sword 10, Dragon Sword 80). Short Sword at STR 20: +234 per update, observed exactly measured.
  • The swing timer advances 300 per update and the hit is tested once, in the update that takes it from 3,000 to 3,300: the 11th update counting the press as the first, at 20 updates a second measured (first in DuckStation, 2026-10-17). The Corissumaldo's test is at 700, the 4th update.
  • The test point is the weapon's reach value (byte f9) ahead of the eye; a monster within 800 units horizontally and 1,000 vertically of it is hit (kf_EntityFindOverlap). Short Sword 750 … Crescent Axe and Flame Sword 1,300; one floor tile is 2,000 units in the code.

Status from hits

The monster first rolls rand() >> 7 < chance, a chance out of 256 (checked over 2,414 real hits: 956 carried a status against 917 expected). Then:

Dark against a floor-3 Phantom: set without the charm, never set with it over 800 updates measured.

How it was checked

The predictions were first tested in 2026-10-16 runs in DuckStation (nine cases, all exact), then re-run in Mednafen on 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-23. Each test puts the game in a real situation (a real monster on floor 1, armour equipped through the real menus), sets only the value under test in memory, and stops the emulator at the damage function with a breakpoint to read its inputs and outputs. Every case produced exactly the predicted number. Not yet measured: weapon reach and the rule that a zero-damage hit does nothing, both read from the code.
