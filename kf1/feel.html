Why it feels heavy

King's Field, read from the code, part 2 · revised 2026-10-23 · draft

In short

Everyone who plays King's Field remembers how it feels: slow, heavy, a little like wading. That feel is made of a few plain numbers in the program:

  • The game thinks 20 times a second, steadily.
  • Walking and turning have momentum: you speed up over a fifth of a second and drift a little when you let go.
  • Turning is slow: a full circle takes about 7.3 seconds.
  • Sidestepping stops sharply, while walking forward drifts.

None of this is an accident of old hardware. The numbers are deliberate and consistent, and some of them, like refusing to let you run faster diagonally, fix mistakes other games of the time made.

How it works

A steady heartbeat

The game waits for at least three screen refreshes between updates, so it runs at 20 updates a second measured. Every speed in the game is counted per update. A dungeon full of textured 3D tiles couldn't reliably be drawn faster on a launch-year PlayStation, and a rate that is always 20 feels deliberate where one that wobbles between 20 and 30 feels like stutter our reading.

Momentum

Pressing forward doesn't put you at full speed at once. Speed builds over 4 updates (0.2 seconds) to a top speed of 1.8 tiles a second measured. Let go and it falls away over about 8 updates, so you drift a step or so in the code. This is much of the weight: a person in armour in the dark, not a camera on rails our reading.

Sidestepping (strafe) builds up just as fast but stops twice as fast as walking forward in the code. In a fight that is what you want: you sidestep a blow and stop exactly where you meant to our reading.

A slow turn

Turning also has momentum, and its top speed is low: about 49 degrees a second, so a full turn takes about 7.3 seconds. We predicted that from the program and then timed it in play measured. You can't spin round when something hits you from behind; you have to face things, and approach rooms carefully. A fast turn at 20 updates a second would also look jerky our reading.

Looking up and down is limited to about 17 degrees either way in the code.

No faster diagonals

In some early 3D games, walking forward and sidestepping at once made you move about 41 % faster than either alone. King's Field explicitly prevents it: when you do both, the game rescales the two speeds so that the total is never more than the faster of the two in the code. It does this with an unusual but cheap formula that needs only one square root our reading.

Your gear works on its own clock

Your weapon and five pieces of equipment can each heal 1 HP, restore 1 MP or drain 1 HP every so many updates, each at its own interval in the code. One number per item gives many items a personality (a ring that regenerates, a cursed piece that slowly bleeds you) without any special code our reading. One piece of armour, the Skull Armor, stops your magic from charging at all in the code.

Magic charges like a breath

The magic gauge works like the sword's: it refills over time, at a rate set by your MAG and the spell, and casting needs both a full gauge and enough MP in the code. MP limits how much magic you use; the gauge limits how often. Together they give fights a rhythm: cast, back off, wait, cast our reading.

From here on it gets technical: formulas, memory addresses and how each claim was checked.

Paralysis divides every limit by 5. Measured: holding forward on flat ground gives 0, 46, 91, 136, 181 then 181 units per update; paralysed, 0, 9, 18, 28, 36 then 36 measured.

kf_WaitFrame (800149f4) waits for at least 3 vertical blanks; on a 60 Hz Japanese console that is 20 updates a second. Slow frames are not compensated in the code. The 7.3-second turn (28 × 20 = 560/4,096 of a turn per second) matches play measured.

Combining forward and strafe

With forward speed f*, strafe speed *s and len = √(f² + s²), the applied components are f² / len and s² / len (keeping their signs). Both at 180 still move 180 units per update, not 254 in the code. Squaring also bends the direction toward the stronger axis. The two components are applied one after the other, forward first, each with its own collision test: the source of wall sliding (A 3D world on a 2D grid).

Head bob

Only while on the ground: phase = (phase + 2 × speed) mod 4096, bob = sin(phase) × 64. At full speed a cycle takes about 11 updates (0.57 s), and the eye height swings by ±64 units, measured as −11,437 … −11,564 measured.

View

77° horizontal field of view (screen distance 200 on a 320-pixel-wide screen), 4:3; lighting is baked into the scenery's vertex colours in the code.

Equipment over time

The weapon and five more slots (head, chest, shield, arms, feet) each carry three "every n* updates" effects: +1 HP, +1 MP, −1 HP; *n = 0 turns an effect off. They count against the frame counter at 800a07d8 in the code. Chest item 0x17 (Skull Armor) blocks the magic charge in the code.

Magic gauge

The gauge rises in every update except the one where the cast button is newly pressed (holding it still charges; a correction of 2026-10-20 to an earlier reading). Casting needs a full gauge (5,000) and MP ≥ the spell's cost; it then spends the MP and resets the gauge to 0 in the code. The Wind Blade Bracelet halves the cost of Wind Cutter in the code.
